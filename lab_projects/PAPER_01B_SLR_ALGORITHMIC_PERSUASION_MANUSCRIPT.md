## ABSTRACT

Artificial intelligence recommendation systems and conversational shopping agents have reshaped digital retail by compressing product discovery and triggering spontaneous consumption. However, existing scholarship remains fragmented across linear adoption models that overlook cognitive resource depletion, endogenous privacy reactance, and recursive post-purchase regret. Following PRISMA 2020 and SPAR-4-SLR protocols, this systematic review synthesizes 1,016 peer-reviewed journal articles published between 2018 and 2026, integrating an expanded OpenAlex harvest with the core Dimensions corpus. Quantitative bibliometric mapping across 29 core constructs and 340 co-occurrence links identifies four thematic clusters, demonstrating that the personalization-privacy paradox, perceived intrusiveness, and artificial intelligence skepticism represent the most recent research frontier (Cluster 2, average publication year = 2025.11). A reproducible text-mining gap audit reveals severe empirical under-exploration of ego depletion (0.79%), multiplicative cognitive-affective interactions (0.98%), inverted-U personalization thresholds (2.17%), and longitudinal panel designs (2.95%). To resolve these theoretical silos, we develop an Integrative Dual-Path Stimulus-Organism-Response framework articulated through nine formal propositions (P1 to P9, alongside mediation paths P4a, P5a, P5b, and P8a). The framework models curvilinear personalization thresholds, contrasts Generation 1.0 collaborative filtering against Generation 2.0 conversational agency, and specifies how post-purchase regret erodes future satisfaction while shifting privacy thresholds leftward.

**Keywords:** Systematic literature review; Artificial intelligence recommendations; Generative AI agents; Online impulse buying; Personalization-privacy paradox; Ego depletion; Stimulus-Organism-Response

## 1. INTRODUCTION

### 1.1 Evolution from Static Recommendation Filtering to Agentic Retail Persuasion

Algorithmic curation has become the primary decision architecture in contemporary electronic commerce. Early recommender systems relied predominantly on Generation 1.0 collaborative filtering and matrix factorization to match historical clickstreams with catalog items. As Xiao and Benbasat (2007) established in their foundational taxonomy of recommendation agents, algorithmic advice shapes consumer decision quality by lowering search costs while simultaneously altering choice heuristics. Over the past three years, however, retail platforms have transitioned toward Generation 2.0 generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) shopping assistants, multimodal livestreaming recommendation engines, and low-latency conversational agents capable of dynamic persuasion.

Recent empirical investigations reflect this structural shift across social commerce, livestreaming marketplaces, and mobile retail applications. For example, Ashfaq et al. (2020), Grewal et al. (2024), Huang and Rust (2022), and Zhang et al. (2022) document how real-time algorithmic cues, interactive video streams, and personalized prompts accelerate affective engagement and spontaneous purchasing. Similarly, Xue et al. (2020), Cheng et al. (2021), and Chen et al. (2022) examine how perceived intelligence, social presence, and algorithmic responsiveness shape consumer trust and purchase intentions in emerging digital ecosystems. Despite rapid output growth, where 81.89% of all included studies (832 of 1,016 articles) appeared between 2024 and 2026, theoretical integration lags behind technological deployment.

### 1.2 Quantitative Gap Audit and Problem Statement

Prior reviews have examined isolated facets of smart retail, chatbot adoption, or social commerce, yet most synthesize small narrative samples without quantifying structural blind spots across the broader literature. To establish an objective empirical baseline, we executed an automated text-mining gap audit across the entire final included corpus ($N = 1,016$). While 122 articles (12.01%) invoke the Stimulus-Organism-Response (S-O-R) paradigm introduced by Mehrabian and Russell (1974), the vast majority specify additive, linear, and cross-sectional pathways.

Specifically, our audit demonstrates that only 22 articles (2.17%) test inverted-U or curvilinear personalization thresholds, despite classic warnings from Xiao and Benbasat (2018) regarding biased or overly intrusive recommendations. Furthermore, only 46 studies (4.53%) examine algorithmic surveillance reactance and privacy paradox dynamics, and merely 10 articles (0.98%) model multiplicative interaction effects between cognitive satisfaction and affective arousal. Most critically, only 8 studies (0.79%) incorporate ego depletion or decision fatigue into AI-mediated impulse buying, and just 30 studies (2.95%) employ longitudinal or cross-lagged panel designs capable of capturing post-purchase regret feedback. Additional recent studies such as Seele et al. (2019), Riegger et al. (2020), and Potwora et al. (2024) highlight the urgency of bridging cognitive, affective, and ethical mechanisms within a unified model.

### 1.3 Research Questions and Objectives

To address these empirical and theoretical asymmetries, this systematic review pursues four research questions:
1. **RQ1 (Publication & Intellectual Structure):** How has scholarly output on AI-driven retail recommendations and consumer impulse behavior evolved from 2018 to 2026 across journals, citation benchmarks, and co-occurrence clusters?
2. **RQ2 (Theoretical & Methodological Maturity):** Which theories, contexts, constructs, and analytical methods dominate the expanded corpus ($N = 1,016$), and where do critical methodological blind spots persist?
3. **RQ3 (Dual-Path & Dark-Side Integration):** How do Generation 1.0 filtering and Generation 2.0 conversational agency jointly influence cognitive fluency, affective arousal, ego depletion, and endogenous surveillance reactance?
4. **RQ4 (Recursive Feedback & Ethical Governance):** How does post-purchase regret feed back to erode future electronic satisfaction (e-satisfaction) and amplify privacy reactance, and how can tiered ethical friction mitigate long-term customer lifetime value erosion?

### 1.4 Overview of Contributions

This review delivers three distinct contributions to the retailing and consumer services literature. First, it expands the empirical evidence base through a transparent multi-database synthesis combining Dimensions and OpenAlex ($N = 1,016$), anchored by reproducible bibliometric network metrics (Occurrences, Total Link Strength, and Average Publication Year) rather than qualitative cluster descriptions alone. Second, it applies the Theories-Contexts-Characteristics-Methods (TCCM) framework popularized by Paul et al. (2021) alongside bibliometric mapping guidelines from Donthu et al. (2021) to diagnose structural research gaps. Third, it advances an Integrative Dual-Path S-O-R Conceptual Framework comprising nine formal theoretical propositions (P1 to P9, supplemented by mediation and boundary specifications P4a, P5a, P5b, and P8a) alongside an explicit empirical testing strategy.

## 2. SYSTEMATIC REVIEW METHODOLOGY (PRISMA 2020 & SPAR-4-SLR)

### 2.1 Search Strategy and Multi-Database Boolean Protocol

We conducted this systematic review in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA 2020) guidelines (Page et al., 2021) and the Scientific Procedures and Rationales for Systematic Literature Reviews (SPAR-4-SLR) protocol (Paul et al., 2021). To avoid confirmation bias toward any single theoretical lens (such as S-O-R or Technology Acceptance Model), we designed an agnostic two-pillar Boolean query targeting technological stimuli (AI recommendation, personalization, generative agents) and behavioral retail outcomes (impulse buying, purchase intention, consumer behavior):

```text
TITLE-ABS-KEY (
  ("artificial intelligence" OR "recommender system*" OR "recommendation agent*"
   OR "algorithmic personalization" OR "generative AI" OR "conversational AI"
   OR "chatbot*" OR "livestream* commerce")
  AND
  ("impulse buying" OR "impulsive purchase" OR "urge to buy impulsively"
   OR "consumer behavior" OR "online shopping" OR "e-commerce" OR "retail")
)
```

Database harvesting combined two complementary bibliographic sources covering 2018 through 2026: (1) an established Dimensions core export ($n = 1,441$ records), and (2) an expanded multi-query OpenAlex harvest ($n = 678$ records) targeting emerging 2024-2026 themes including generative shopping assistants, algorithmic privacy reactance, ego depletion, and livestreaming commerce. Consistent with methodological recommendations from Hollebeek et al. (2024), Ajiga et al. (2024), and Huang et al. (2024), combining structured metadata registries increases coverage of recent open-access and interdisciplinary retail scholarship.

### 2.2 Deduplication and Deterministic Eligibility Screening

Merging both data streams yielded 2,119 gross records. Automated normalization of Digital Object Identifiers (DOIs) and punctuation-stripped titles removed 92 duplicate entries, leaving 2,027 unique records for title and abstract screening. We then applied three deterministic exclusion criteria:
* **EC1 (Non-Journal Outlets):** Conference proceedings, workshops, book chapters, and unrefereed preprints were excluded ($n = 65$), ensuring that only peer-reviewed journal articles entered the synthesis.
* **EC2 (Off-Topic Scope):** Articles lacking explicit intersection between consumer retail/shopping contexts and AI/algorithmic/digital recommendation cues were excluded ($n = 922$).
* **EC3 (Metadata & Window Completeness):** Records published outside the 2018-2026 window or containing insufficient abstract text (< 35 words) for reliable text-mining were excluded ($n = 24$).

In total, 1,011 records were excluded during screening, yielding a final included corpus of **$N = 1,016$ peer-reviewed journal articles**. Prior reviews in related domains, such as Bunea et al. (2024), and Yin et al. (2025), emphasize that transparent funnel accounting is essential for auditability. Figure 1 summarizes the complete PRISMA 2020 selection workflow.

### 2.3 Reproducible Text-Mining Gap Audit and Bibliometric Protocol

To eliminate subjective gap assertions, we implemented a deterministic Python text-mining audit across all $N = 1,016$ included records. For each article, the lowercased title and abstract were concatenated and matched against curated keyword families representing seven methodological and theoretical dimensions:
1. *S-O-R Framework:* `stimulus-organism-response`, `s-o-r`, `sor model`, `sor framework`.
2. *Generative AI & Shopping Agents:* `generative ai`, `genai`, `large language model`, `llm`, `chatgpt`, `ai shopping assistant`.
3. *Surveillance Reactance & Privacy Paradox:* `surveillance`, `reactance`, `intrusiveness`, `privacy paradox`.
4. *Longitudinal & Panel Designs:* `longitudinal`, `panel data`, `cross-lagged`, `multi-wave`, `experience sampling`.
5. *Inverted-U & Curvilinear Thresholds:* `inverted-u`, `inverted u`, `curvilinear`, `quadratic`, `non-linear`, `nonlinear`.
6. *Multiplicative Interactions:* `moderated mediation`, `multiplicative`, `three-way interaction`, `interaction term`.
7. *Ego Depletion & Decision Fatigue:* `ego depletion`, `decision fatigue`, `self-regulatory depletion`, `cognitive depletion`.

For science mapping, we constructed a domain-standardized construct co-occurrence network across 29 core concepts appearing in at least 10 articles, retaining co-occurrence links with weight $\ge 3$ (340 total links). Community detection used weighted greedy modularity optimization, and node metrics (Occurrences, Links, Total Link Strength [TLS], and Average Publication Year [APY]) were exported in VOSviewer-compatible JSON format following Donthu et al. (2021).

### 3.3 TCCM Synthesis of Existing Literature

Applying the TCCM framework (Paul et al., 2021) reveals four structural asymmetries across the 1,016-article corpus (Table 2). Theoretically, S-O-R (12.01%) and Technology Acceptance extensions dominate, whereas self-regulation theories (Baumeister, 2002; Baumeister et al., 2007) and cognitive load frameworks (Sweller, 1988) remain scarce (0.79%). ### 4.1 Curvilinear Personalization and Generation 2.0 Conversational Agency (P1-P2)

Prior retail literature frequently models Perceived Personalization (PP) as monotonically beneficial for consumer satisfaction. Drawing on privacy calculus and expectation-confirmation logic (Oliver, 1980; Xiao & Benbasat, 2018), we argue that personalization enhances cognitive fluency up to an optimal threshold, beyond which excessive data specificity triggers perceived surveillance and cognitive discomfort. Meanwhile, Generation 2.0 conversational AI agents differ qualitatively from Generation 1.0 collaborative filtering by exhibiting anthropomorphic social agency, natural-language persuasion, and micro-latency responsiveness, as documented by Aydin (2026), Peltier et al. (2024), and Hennighausen et al. (2025).

* **Proposition 1 (P1):** Perceived Personalization (PP) exerts an inverted-U curvilinear effect on Cognitive Fluency and E-Satisfaction, such that personalization improves fluency at moderate levels but degrades fluency once algorithmic specificity crosses the consumer's privacy comfort threshold.
* **Proposition 2 (P2):** Generation 2.0 conversational AI agency generates stronger social presence and micro-latency affective arousal than Generation 1.0 static collaborative filtering, accelerating spontaneous evaluation shifts.

### 4.2 Algorithmic Scarcity, Cognitive Load, and Ego Depletion (P3-P4a)

When AI recommenders combine high-velocity product carousels with dynamic scarcity prompts, consumers experience elevated cognitive load (Sweller, 1988). Consistent with the strength model of self-control (Baumeister, 2002; Baumeister et al., 2007), sustained resistance against tailored persuasion depletes finite executive willpower, even accounting for boundary debates noted by Hagger et al. (2016). Recent e-commerce evidence from Adyantari et al. (2025), Amin (2025), and Yang et al. (2024) supports the link between cognitive overload and impaired deliberation.

* **Proposition 3 (P3):** High-velocity algorithmic scarcity cues and recommendation choice volume increase Cognitive Load, positively driving Ego Depletion during prolonged shopping sessions.
* **Proposition 4 (P4):** In-session Cognitive Fluency positively predicts immediate E-Satisfaction (ESAT) for both new (cold-start) and returning shoppers by lowering decision friction.
* **Proposition 4a (P4a):** Ego Depletion mediates the relationship between algorithmic choice overload and heightened Affective Arousal, weakening deliberative restraint prior to checkout.

### 4.3 Dual-Path Mediation and Multiplicative Interaction (P5, P5a, P5b)

A persistent limitation in S-O-R studies is modeling cognitive satisfaction and affective arousal as independent additive predictors of impulse urges (Rook & Fisher, 1995; Beatty & Ferrell, 1998). In practice, high emotional excitement rarely converts into unplanned checkout if the consumer distrusts the interface, whereas cognitive comfort alone lacks kinetic urgency. We therefore specify a multiplicative permission-energy mechanism.

* **Proposition 5 (P5):** The Urge to Buy Impulsively (UBI) serves as the proximate organismic trigger converting dual-path organismic states into Online Impulse Buying (OIB).
* **Proposition 5a (P5a):** Cognitive E-Satisfaction (ESAT) and Affective Arousal jointly mediate the influence of AI stimuli on UBI.
* **Proposition 5b (P5b):** ESAT and Affective Arousal interact multiplicatively (ESAT $\times$ Affective Arousal $\rightarrow$ UBI), such that UBI peaks when cognitive fluency disarms evaluative vigilance alongside elevated affective arousal.

### 4.4 Transparency, Self-Regulation, and Endogenous Surveillance Reactance (P6-P8a)

Algorithmic transparency and consumer self-regulation act as critical guardrails against manipulative persuasion. Furthermore, rather than treating privacy concern as a static exogenous trait, our framework models Surveillance Reactance as endogenously activated when Perceived Personalization exceeds the consumer's privacy threshold (P1).

* **Proposition 6 (P6):** Algorithmic Transparency positively moderates the relationship between Perceived Personalization and Cognitive Fluency, flattening the downward slope of the inverted-U curve.
* **Proposition 7 (P7):** AI Skepticism and Trait Self-Control negatively moderate the translation of Affective Arousal and Ego Depletion into UBI, buffering vulnerable shoppers from unplanned spending.
* **Proposition 8 (P8):** Beyond the privacy threshold of Perceived Personalization, consumers experience endogenous Surveillance Reactance, which directly suppresses Cognitive Fluency and E-Satisfaction.
* **Proposition 8a (P8a):** Surveillance Reactance mediates the negative tail of the inverted-U personalization curve, explaining why hyper-accurate targeting frequently provokes cart abandonment.

### 4.5 Recursive Post-Purchase Regret Feedback Loop (P9)

Cross-sectional designs implicitly treat each impulse purchase as an isolated terminal outcome. However, once executive self-control recovers after checkout, OIB driven by ego depletion frequently generates cognitive dissonance, post-purchase regret, and product returns. As Figure 3 depicts via the dashed feedback path, this regret propagates backward into subsequent shopping sessions.

* **Proposition 9 (P9):** Online Impulse Buying executed under high Ego Depletion elevates Post-Purchase Regret, which recursively erodes future cumulative E-Satisfaction and amplifies future Surveillance Reactance by shifting the consumer's inverted-U privacy tolerance threshold leftward in subsequent sessions.

### 4.6 Empirical Model Testing Strategy

To ensure empirical testability of Propositions P1 through P9, future researchers should avoid single-wave linear PLS-SEM alone. First, **P1 and P8/P8a** should be tested using **Polynomial Regression with Response Surface Analysis (RSA)** or quadratic latent variable terms ($PP^2$) in structural equation modeling to locate exact inflection points. Second, **P5b** requires **latent moderated structural equations (LMS)** or orthogonalized two-stage product indicators to estimate the multiplicative term ($ESAT \times Arousal$). Third, **P9** demands **longitudinal Cross-Lagged Panel Models (CLPM)** or multi-wave experience sampling across at least three consecutive shopping episodes ($T_1 \rightarrow T_2 \rightarrow T_3$) so that post-purchase regret at $T_1$ can be modeled as a predictor of baseline E-Satisfaction and shifted personalization thresholds at $T_2$.

## 5. DISCUSSION, IMPLICATIONS, AND LIMITATIONS

### 5.1 Theoretical Contributions

This review advances digital retailing theory in three substantive ways. First, by expanding the systematic corpus to $N = 1,016$ articles and executing a reproducible text-mining audit, we quantify the exact empirical scarcity of ego depletion (0.79%), curvilinear thresholds (2.17%), and longitudinal designs (2.95%). Second, our quantitative VOSviewer analysis proves that Cluster 2 (privacy, intrusiveness, and AI skepticism; mean APY = 2025.11) has emerged as the newest intellectual frontier, overtaking traditional trust-adoption studies. Third, our Integrative Dual-Path S-O-R Framework bridges information systems architecture (Xiao & Benbasat, 2007, 2018) with self-regulation psychology (Baumeister, 2002), demonstrating how short-term conversion gains from ego depletion can cannibalize long-term platform equity via P9.

### 5.2 Managerial Implications and Tiered Ethical Friction

For retail executives, platform architects, and UX designers, the findings caution against maximizing short-term conversion through aggressive scarcity prompts and hyper-granular retargeting. As Nguyen et al. (2025), Pham et al. (2024), and Jiao et al. (2024) observe, short-term algorithmic nudges that erode consumer trust impose hidden costs through higher return rates and churn. Platforms should implement **Tiered Ethical Friction**: for low-regret utilitarian staples, conversational AI agents can streamline one-click checkout; however, for high-ticket or high-regret discretionary categories where algorithmic velocity detects elevated arousal and choice fatigue, platforms should introduce calibrated cooling-off nudges, transparent "why you see this" explanations (P6), and adjustable personalization sliders to prevent crossing the P1/P8 surveillance threshold.

### 5.3 Future Research Agenda

Building on the TCCM gaps in Table 2 and recent directions highlighted by Kolar et al. (2024), and Wasiq et al. (2024), future scholarship should prioritize: (1) experimental field trials comparing Generation 1.0 collaborative filtering against Generation 2.0 multimodal LLM agents under controlled cognitive load; (2) longitudinal panel tracking of post-purchase regret and product return cascades (P9); and (3) cross-cultural replication of privacy thresholds across Western and Asian livestreaming markets.

### 5.4 Limitations and Conclusion

In accordance with PRISMA 2020 Item 23c (Page et al., 2021), several limitations bound this review. First, applying **EC1 (exclusion of conference proceedings)** ensured peer-reviewed journal rigor across all 1,016 included articles, yet it necessarily omitted cutting-edge computer science workshops and flagship recommender conferences (such as ACM RecSys, SIGIR, KDD, and CHI) where novel GenAI architectures often debut prior to journal publication. Second, our reproducible text-mining gap audit operated on titles and abstracts rather than full-text PDFs; consequently, studies that tested quadratic or interaction terms solely in robustness tables without mentioning them in the abstract may be conservatively undercounted. Third, restricting inclusion to English-language journal outlets may underrepresent regional insights from non-English livestreaming markets. Despite these boundaries, this review provides a transparent, quantitatively grounded foundation and an actionable theoretical roadmap for understanding how AI-enabled retail balances persuasive personalization against consumer cognitive welfare.
